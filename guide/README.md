# Using PathLMS

This is the guide to using PathLMS. It is written to teach, not to list features.
Every task starts with why you would do it and what it gets you, then shows you
the screen and the exact steps.

## Find your guide

PathLMS gives each person one of five roles, and what you see depends on which
one you hold. Start with the guide for your role.

- **[The learner guide](learner.md)**. You are here to take training. Find a
  course, work through it, pass a quiz, see what you have finished, and print a
  certificate.
- **[The author guide](author.md)**. You build the material. Make a course, fill
  it with lessons and quizzes, and decide how it is put together.
- **[The manager guide](manager.md)**. You get training to people. Organize
  people into groups, enroll them, set training that repeats, and read who is on
  track and who is behind.
- **[The administrator guide](administrator.md)**. You run the platform. Manage
  accounts and roles, set up company sign-in, brand it, and keep it backed up and
  up to date.

Two guides sit across the roles because they are the heart of how PathLMS is put
together:

- **[Courses, folders and learning paths](courses-folders-and-paths.md)**. The
  most important guide. How a course, a folder of courses, and a learning path
  fit together, and how you build and look after each one.
- **[The glossary](glossary.md)**. How to give a word the right meaning inside
  the right course, so a learner understands a term without leaving the page.

## A note on the roles

- **Administrator** can do everything.
- **Manager** looks after people and gets training to them, for the groups they
  run.
- **Author** builds courses and paths.
- **Learner** takes training.
- **Instructor** is a role you can assign, but today it works exactly like a
  learner. There is nothing it does that a learner does not, so if you are an
  instructor, follow the learner guide.

## How to read these guides

Each task is laid out the same way. First, **why you would do it**, in a line or
two. Then a **picture of the screen** you will be on. Then **the steps**, one
action at a time, naming the exact button. Where there is a good way and a poor
way to do something, the guide says which and why, so you are not left guessing.

You do not need to read these in order. Find the task you have in front of you
and go straight to it.
